# Goal rewards and adaptive time

Open World offers **Goal rewards & adaptive time** in the room exercise panel.

The controls affect selection fitness and the finite episode deadline. They do not alter the imported neural weights or add a reward input. Re-evolve an offspring under the new rules and review held-out results before adopting it.

**Bonus for reaching the goal nose-first** rewards an actual forward arrival with the target within 45° of the nose. Reversing remains valid and still earns the ordinary collection reward. Stationary poses and sight-only confirmations earn no nose bonus. The default UI bonus is 30 points; old imported setups keep this feature disabled unless their metadata enables it. Extra orientation points are capped at 200 per trial in selection scores, preserving completion priority over unsuccessful runs.

**Adaptive time** extends a finite trial after each collected target except the last. Defaults add 10 seconds after goal 1, 15 seconds after goal 2, then complete a three-goal trial on goal 3. The base time budget is unchanged until a goal is earned. Choose 1–6 targets or a reproducible random count; visual search's preset default becomes three when adaptive time is enabled. Repeated frames and already collected targets cannot extend time again. Live continuation starts another bounded target set; ghost evolution and held-out tests share the same settings and seeded placement rules. All ghosts share the initial target. Later visual flags are sampled from the same seeded stream relative to each robot's current pose, so their coordinates can differ after different trajectories.

Visual search shows **one flag at a time**. A sight-only trial collects it after two fresh processed-camera confirmations; a reach trial requires confirmation and actual arrival inside the selected yellow radius. The next flag is newly sampled outside the robot's current field of view, retaining recent-position exclusions. Target image cues reset to zero and frames from the previous target are ignored. No compass, hidden target distance, map or reward channel is added to the controller. This is an explicit pink-colour detector, not a trained object recognizer.

Partial successful visual collections earn a bounded selection score, while complete searches keep priority. Successful multi-target time ranking uses completion time divided by the extended deadline, rather than the first sighting time. Per-trial results record collection count, nose reward, extended deadline and earned ticks. Brain/setup/scene exports and generation provenance retain the rules; plateau recovery treats a change of reward rules as a different experiment. These checks validate episode plumbing, not a claim that a particular saved brain has learned to navigate reliably.
