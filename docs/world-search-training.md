# Open-world circling and exploration training

Visual search formerly gave an unsuccessful candidate up to 1.28 points for distinct coarse camera views. A small moving circle can collect that bonus. Visual stall detection does not catch this reliably because a turning camera sees motion. Compass-trained or racer-derived controllers can also retain a turning habit when their mission inputs change to pixel bearing and size (both zero before seeing the target). These are mechanisms that can produce the behavior, not a measured diagnosis of every saved checkpoint.

The **Exploration progress** judge is enabled by default. A first visit to each approximately 35 cm ground cell supplies bounded coverage credit in unsuccessful visual-search fitness (up to 20 points). Repeated cells and heading changes supply no new coverage. A new eligible cell, collected goal or meaningful increase in apparent target size resets the no-progress clock. Compass exercises may also reset it with improving compass goal progress; visual exercises never read an unseen goal's distance.

The default loop guard checks a rolling 24-second route window for substantial travel and turning with little displacement inside a small area. It works in either turning direction, and reclaims credit banked inside a confirmed local orbit. Reverse visual search earns no new-ground credit unless the observed target is actually getting closer in the image. Brief backing escapes still have the normal grace period; explicit reverse/compass exercises remain valid. These are configurable training heuristics: slow inspection or a legitimate tight detour can resemble a loop.

After six seconds without progress, negative reward starts at 0.5 points/second and judge pain at 0.04 units/second. Both double every four seconds, capped at eight times their initial rate and scaled by the chosen weight. Grace, cutoff and weight are configurable. Brief turns and reversing remain valid. Successful searches always outrank failures; their explicit no-progress deduction is capped at 200 points. Coverage credit is for failed searches only, so finding quickly remains the objective.

**Zero chill** is optional. It stops and disqualifies a trial after a contact or twelve seconds without progress. Disqualified candidates cannot breed or autosave. If no eligible candidate remains (including forward-retention checks), the exact previous parent stays in place. Reports distinguish attempted generations from the last accepted winner generation, preserve rejection reasons and include coverage, pain, cost and judge configuration. Brain exports/imports preserve the settings; older brains receive the new default judge configuration without changing any weights.

Judge pain stays separate from physical impact pain, so it cannot accidentally trigger a fatal-collision threshold. These labels affect evolutionary fitness and are logged, rather than changing synapses online. Existing circling weights need new evolution with the selected judge; enabling it alone does not teach a loaded brain to search.

Coverage uses simulator body position as an external training label. It is not a new neural input, perfect navigation memory or a sensor available on an ESP32. The deployed controller continues to receive its configured camera, sonar and body-feedback inputs. Real-world progress diagnosis needs measured odometry/visual motion or contact hardware; the existing camera-based stall detector remains separate. No physical transfer benefit or convergence improvement is claimed without evaluation.

## Inspecting and breeding controllers

Open World connects front sonar by default, extending a legacy 17-input controller to 19 with zero-initialized sonar weights. The inherited 17 channels are preserved. The unchanged parent competes with local mutations and donor crossovers on identical trials. Donors can be imported from JSON, selected from repository checkpoints or added from a saved generation while training; changes apply at the next generation boundary. Crossover mixes complete neuron units, then mutates them. All offspring use the currently selected camera head and fresh independent memory.

Select a coloured cart or use **Inspect cart** to see that candidate's FPV, sonar, neural activity and controls. **Export inspected brain** exports its actual candidate weights, rather than the parent used to construct the observer. The generation graphs show selected/mean score, success rate and qualified exploration credit. Click a generation to inspect its saved weights; rejected generations retain statistics but do not become saved winners.

The persistent generation tree keeps every accepted controller, its parent, donor sources, original eyes/setup, training context and milestone status. Donor records retain original and remapped weights. Export/import the whole tree to move it between browsers; importing merges without replacing the driving brain. Each completed generation autosaves before the worker continues. If browser storage is unavailable/full, snapshots remain in the tab and the UI asks you to save the tree.

Automatic recovery watches training trends, not held-out outcomes. After the configured number of generations without a meaningful score or success-rate improvement, it breeds again from a retained milestone, changes the mutation seed, increases mutation strength and adds an unrelated forward-search scout. Later plateaus try earlier milestones. Recovery state, including a queued rollback, carries over to another evolution run with the same parent and context. Fresh-trial scores are noisy; this is a diversification heuristic rather than proof of convergence or biological inbreeding.

## Preparing for the real cart

New sessions default to **Motor command estimate · no encoders**. Input 11 integrates issued forward/reverse/brake commands, with no access to true speed, position, collisions or terrain. It keeps estimating motion when a wheel is blocked, as an uninstrumented real cart would. **Measured speed** uses simulator speed and requires a corresponding encoder or calibrated speed sensor on hardware. Legacy imports without this field retain measured mode, and show that choice explicitly. Both modes must be calibrated against the actual chassis; simulator speed units are not physical PWM calibration.

New sessions also default to **HC-SR04 · range + echo-valid**. Channel 18 becomes a binary detected-echo bit rather than simulated analogue amplitude; the same choice reaches sonar-conditioned camera heads. Raw acoustic strength remains available to the echo model and observer diagnostics, and surfaces still affect detection. Existing files without this setting retain legacy amplitude mode explicitly. Amplitude-trained controllers and camera heads need retraining for the new channel meaning. The basic module exposes trigger/echo pulse timing, not analogue strength ([manufacturer datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Sensors/Proximity/HCSR04.pdf)).

Every training checkpoint/report records a simulation transfer contract: camera dimensions/colour, speed source, sonar connection, target-cue source, training-only labels and remaining calibration requirements. Explore mode uses processed-camera target cues; compass exercises require a real localization source. A front camera/HC-SR04 cannot establish rear clearance. The 2D sprite camera, vehicle geometry, echo-strength model and motor response remain approximations; export does not certify ESP32 readiness or automatically arm hardware.

Frozen paired held-out trials block driving adoption if success count drops, disqualification/fatal-crash count rises, or average score regresses by more than 20 points. Reverse practice also retains its independent forward check. Training snapshots stay available even if adoption fails. Two validation trials are a small simulation check, not a hardware safety or generalization guarantee. Before physical use, test unseen rooms, camera/sonar failures and lighting, calibrate measured dimensions/timing/braking in the 3D stage, and use the existing independent motor stop guard with supervised low-speed trials.
