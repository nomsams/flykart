# Open-world circling and exploration training

Visual search formerly gave an unsuccessful candidate up to 1.28 points for distinct coarse camera views. A small moving circle can collect that bonus. Visual stall detection does not catch this reliably because a turning camera sees motion. Compass-trained or racer-derived controllers can also retain a turning habit when their mission inputs change to pixel bearing and size (both zero before seeing the target). These are mechanisms that can produce the behavior, not a measured diagnosis of every saved checkpoint.

The **Exploration progress** judge is enabled by default. A first visit to each approximately 35 cm ground cell supplies bounded coverage credit in unsuccessful visual-search fitness (up to 20 points). Repeated cells and heading changes supply no new coverage. A new cell, collected goal or meaningful increase in apparent target size resets the no-progress clock. Compass exercises may also reset it with improving compass goal progress; visual exercises never read an unseen goal's distance.

After six seconds without progress, negative reward starts at 0.5 points/second and judge pain at 0.04 units/second. Both double every four seconds, capped at eight times their initial rate and scaled by the chosen weight. Grace, cutoff and weight are configurable. Brief turns and reversing remain valid. Successful searches always outrank failures; their explicit no-progress deduction is capped at 200 points. Coverage credit is for failed searches only, so finding quickly remains the objective.

**Zero chill** is optional. It stops and disqualifies a trial after a contact or twelve seconds without progress. Disqualified candidates cannot breed or autosave. If no eligible candidate remains (including forward-retention checks), the exact previous parent stays in place. Reports distinguish attempted generations from the last accepted winner generation, preserve rejection reasons and include coverage, pain, cost and judge configuration. Brain exports/imports preserve the settings; older brains receive the new default judge configuration without changing any weights.

Judge pain stays separate from physical impact pain, so it cannot accidentally trigger a fatal-collision threshold. These labels affect evolutionary fitness and are logged, rather than changing synapses online. Existing circling weights need new evolution with the selected judge; enabling it alone does not teach a loaded brain to search.

Coverage uses simulator body position as an external training label. It is not a new neural input, perfect navigation memory or a sensor available on an ESP32. The deployed controller continues to receive its configured camera, sonar and body-feedback inputs. Real-world progress diagnosis needs measured odometry/visual motion or contact hardware; the existing camera-based stall detector remains separate. No physical transfer benefit or convergence improvement is claimed without evaluation.
