Arrival, stalls and robot transfer

In Open world → Explore & find, check Require arrival inside the yellow area. A sighting alone then cannot win. Two fresh camera frames must confirm the flag and the robot centre must enter the chosen arrival radius (4.4–26.4 cm). The displayed and camera-rendered yellow ring use that radius. The true distance is an external scoring condition; it is not sent to the visual-search brain. Unchecking restores sight-only competition. Save/import retains the objective and radius.

A transferable stall diagnostic

Track → Training & sensor experiments → Visual stall detection enables an experimental observer for both track and room driving/evolution. It is off by default. The observer uses fresh RGB frames, sent throttle/reverse/brake commands, optional front sonar and timestamps. It never reads simulator pose, true speed, collisions or goal position.

RGB is pooled into an 8×4 luminance grid. The observer compares spatial image changes after subtracting uniform exposure shifts. Sustained drive demand (at least 0.35, with brake below 0.2), small changes and useful texture support a suspected stall. Repeated stable echoes within 20 cm can corroborate a featureless facing wall while driving forward. Reverse has no rear-sonar evidence. After the confirmation delay, a binary STALL bit=1 appears in copyable sensor logs. Stale/invalid/dark frames, intended stops and uninformative images suppress the diagnostic; fresh movement resets it.

This is image-change evidence, not full optical flow, proof of contact or measured odometry. Moving people can hide a stall; a textureless far wall can hide movement. Low light, camera noise, wheel vibration, exposure changes and mounting affect the thresholds. Validate against labelled real-camera recordings. OpenCV’s optical-flow guide describes image-motion estimation and its brightness assumptions.

Growing training pain, with limits

For confirmed stall duration t, the pain rate is min(cap, 0.08 × 2^((t − delay) / doublingSeconds)). Defaults: 1.2 s confirmation, doubling every 2 s, cap 1 pain/s. The rate is integrated on the 30 Hz simulation clock, regardless of display FPS. Track scoring subtracts 12 points per pain unit; compass-room scoring uses accumulated pain in its safety term. Visual-search scoring additionally subtracts 12 points per stall-pain unit. At 8 accumulated room pain, the simulated run stops. Lower-CPU modes retain these rules.

Existing 17/19-input brains keep their input meanings. The stall bit is currently a diagnostic and an external fitness signal, not an added neural input. A future versioned sensor contract should append separate stall and contact channels with initially zero weights, preserve old checkpoints, and train offspring to use them. Do not relabel existing sonar, goal or motor channels as pain.

Recommended hardware path

Keep three concepts separate: collision event, attempted drive without ground movement, and motor stall. A vibration switch can report a shake but may be quiet against a wall and may trigger on rough flooring. A bumper is clearer contact evidence. Wheel encoders can confirm wheel rotation but not whether spinning wheels move the chassis; camera ground motion helps detect that distinction.

For motor protection, calibrated current monitoring can identify abnormal load; ignore starting transients and use measured thresholds and a timeout independent of the learned policy. TI’s brushed-DC stall-detection application brief explains current-based detection. The existing L298N chassis does not acquire integrated current telemetry merely by enabling this checkbox.

Recommendation: train mixed forward/reverse escapes and unrelated navigation trials, retain the unchanged parent as a benchmark, randomize sensor noise/dropouts and reject offspring that regress on held-out rooms. Use bounded motor-stop/recovery handling on the real robot; a large learning penalty is not a hardware motor safeguard. No physical stall or motor measurements have been made by this implementation.
