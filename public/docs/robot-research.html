Training evidence and marked-floor calibration

The research workbench uses the actual Three.js camera, imported controller, sensor noise, wiring and applied sketch. Simulator truth is reserved for scoring and plots. It is never used as a fly sensory input. A deliberately labelled virtual tracker is available only in calibration demonstrations.

1. Benchmarks and curricula

Choose fixed seeds, trial duration and ablations: current settings, no visual memory, no sonar, single gaze, three gazes, or five gazes with normalization, spatial smoothing and four-frame averaging. Each candidate starts with fresh visual memory and frozen sugar weights. Sensor-noise seeds match within a comparison. Source sketch, eyes, controller, configuration, derived cases and results are included in the report.

Goal-task score is objective reward + 20 if successful − 0.05 × elapsed seconds − 2 × contacts − blocked seconds. Coverage does not reward wandering during a sugar task. A goal ends the trial; time to goal is reported separately. Exploration retains the original coverage/distance score. A sonar-disabled robot has no echo input or sonar-based avoidance.

The curriculum compares three controllers over two training seeds, selects one, and checks two unseen seeds before advancing. The threshold is 80%, which requires both validation trials to succeed. This small validation set is a progression gate, not evidence of a general success rate. Living-room clutter is used for training; office clutter is held out. Trials run sequentially to limit laptop load. Cancellation retains completed results and restores the original lab.

The benchmark table reports paired score differences and a 95% seeded bootstrap interval when at least three matched pairs exist. Trials on the same room family are correlated; the interval is exploratory. CPU time measures capture and controller ticks, excludes deliberate UI yields, and depends on the machine/browser. More Kenyon cells or virtual flies do not establish an improvement.

2. Reward labels and visual places

During live learning, eligibility traces follow the PWM actually issued after the sketch and any enabled wheel compensation. A sketch that ignores a requested turn does not teach that requested turn. The three-choice sugar policy remains a limited left/forward/right association table, not a biological reward circuit or a general reinforcement-learning algorithm. Reverse maneuvers share a turn class. Idle commands create no new action trace.

Visual places use 24 colour-grid features, estimated poses and covariance. Low-detail views are rejected. A view must match repeatedly with a margin over the next candidate. Relative visual anchors have a conservative uncertainty floor and an innovation gate. Place graphs contain at most 256 nodes and undirected observed transitions. Sugar labels attach to the current visual place; optional return navigation uses graph anchors, never sugar ground-truth coordinates. Identical-looking corners can still alias. This graph is not SLAM, and its anchors are correlated.

3. Mark a real robot

  1. Choose a level floor, clear the complete intended path, and note the battery voltage, wheel diameter, mounting geometry, floor material and PWM. Pair motors by side as shown in the wiring diagram.
  2. Put a tape cross beneath the midpoint of the two wheel-centre lines, then draw a forward arrow. This is the origin. +X follows that arrow, +Z points right, and positive heading turns clockwise. Measure from this same fixed origin throughout the run.
  3. Run forward and reverse separately from a fresh cross. Measure the stopped axle midpoint and heading. Repeat each direction at least three times. Use the out-back run to check return-to-origin error and reversal drift. Repeat at another speed to check deadband, latency, sag and nonlinear response. Commands start from rest at each mark.
  4. Run separate left and right 90° pivots from a fresh origin, measuring translation as well as heading. Repeat at least three times each. Left-right and right-left turn-pair runs check heading return and turn-order asymmetry.
  5. Run clockwise and counterclockwise squares. Each square has eight stopped marks: four straight legs and four turns. Measure every corner and turn, including the return to the origin. Inspect target error, predicted-versus-measured error and final heading error.
  6. Fit the drivetrain from straight/reverse/pivot records, then use squares and a different PWM as validation. Contact and PID runs are excluded from the gain fit. Do not mix simulated marks with physical marks; physical data takes precedence.

Connect the serial bridge and verify its reported pin map before a physical floor segment. Each click explicitly arms one bounded pulse (PWM ≤120; duration ≤30 seconds). It stops before requesting the next measurement. Leaving the tab, STOP, cancellation, serial loss, stale sensor frames and the independent chip command watchdog stop motors. Measurements are entered by the user; this app cannot see tape marks in the real room.

Intended targets, command predictions and measurements are three distinct tracks. Simulation demo gains are hidden from fitting and affect only the simulated plant. They model wheel imbalance, not contact dynamics or motor electrical current. A measured mark is required before continuing a physical square. The current timed-pulse model neglects coast distance after stopping; include that in the real stopped measurement and use repeated validation to assess the resulting residual.

4. What the fit identifies

Separate forward/reverse wheel gains are estimated from straight-run arc length and heading. Left/right effective turn scales come from measured pivots after wheel gain correction. The measured centre-line track width is held fixed. Without encoders, command endpoints cannot uniquely separate wheel circumference, supply sag, gearbox dynamics, track width and traction. The result is an effective model at the measured conditions, not a newly discovered physical motor constant. Robust medians reduce the influence of outliers; repeat-count warnings remain visible. Endpoint residuals provide conservative noise scales, not a rigorous identification of time-varying process noise.

Optional inverse gains compensate simulated motor commands and tethered USB-host commands. Identification pulses remain uncompensated. That same post-compensation issued PWM is the distillation target, so the onboard student must not apply the gain correction a second time. The command estimator always predicts from issued commands. Exported calibration includes marks, their source, motor/configuration metadata and measurement uncertainty.

5. Kalman filtering and PID

The extended Kalman estimator predicts X, Z and heading from signed wheel commands, motor lag and calibrated turn scales. Covariance grows with motion. Pose observations use positive position/heading uncertainties, wrapped heading innovations, Mahalanobis outlier gating and a Joseph-form covariance update. This follows the distinction between process models and uncertain measured sensor inputs discussed in the robot_localization sensor-fusion paper.

A surveyed wall is entered as a unit normal and plane offset. A face-on HC-SR04 return constrains distance to that wall, including forward sensor offset. Unknown objects, angled returns and missing echoes are not pose fixes. One range cannot determine full position and heading; lateral uncertainty remains. The workbench applies the sonar scale/offset only to this explicitly surveyed reference; it does not silently invent corrected truth for every obstacle.

Heading PID has editable Kp, Ki, Kd and correction limits. It includes derivative smoothing, an output limit and anti-windup. It requires a fresh measured heading; command prediction alone is not feedback. In simulation calibration, the virtual tracker supplies explicitly labelled truth. In physical calibration, an external tracker may transmit POSE x z headingRadians sigmaMetres sigmaRadians over the same USB log stream. Samples older than 0.5 seconds yield zero correction and reset the integrator. Without such a tracker, floor marks are occasional Kalman corrections and physical runs remain open-loop. PID cannot remove unobserved drift. Wheel-speed PID requires encoders or another actual velocity sensor; this four-wheel kit has none specified.

For sonar calibration, measure several flat target distances spanning the range; record missing echoes explicitly. The offset/scale fit reports residual scatter and dropout. For horizontal camera FOV, use a centred object of known width, perpendicular to the optical axis, at a measured distance, and measure its width in the raw 160-pixel image. FOV calibration does not identify distortion, pitch or automatic-exposure response.

6. Replay and deployment

Replay scrubs frame IDs, raw input crops, processed fly crops, sonar, neural estimates, gaze motor votes, bounded active Kenyon IDs, rewards, requested commands and issued motor outputs. It plots command odometry, filtered estimated pose and privileged simulation truth separately. Camera retention is bounded to six million decoded bytes per recording; omitted frames are explicit. Live recordings retain about one minute of 30 Hz steps. Research keeps four replay episodes and up to 8,000 distillation rows.

The compact student consumes 24 mean RGB features from eight 40×40 blocks in a central 160×80 crop, sonar closeness/validity, previous left/right issued PWM, and magenta cue bearing/strength. Pixels are quantized to RGB565 identically in the browser recipe and generated firmware. A 30→12→2 tanh network is trained on executed motor labels, then quantized to 398 int8 parameters and two per-layer scales. Whole episode groups are held out; float and quantized mean absolute PWM error and sign agreement are reported. Re-importing the same replay uses a retained episode group (a content-derived fallback for legacy files) to avoid dividing duplicates across training and validation.

This student replaces the large perception network and Kenyon table. Parameter storage is 406 bytes; inference uses 44 float activations (176 bytes), excluding stack, camera buffers, runtime and drivers. This is not the full fly connectome or full imported spiking controller. The ESP32 datasheet gives chip memory limits; a successful firmware compile reports actual flash and static RAM usage.

Download the generated flykart_student.ino, save it inside a matching flykart_student folder, and compile for esp32:esp32:esp32cam with PSRAM enabled. Arduino-ESP32 3.x uses the current pin-based LEDC APIs. Verify camera byte order from a known colour before export. Use the existing binary review/flash panel, then reconnect. The monitor must report the same student checksum and GPIO map before an explicit onboard AUTO click.

Onboard runs are capped at 60 seconds and PWM 120. They stop for camera failure, sonar below 12 cm or missing echo (when sonar is enabled), deadline or watchdog expiration. They boot disarmed, support STOP, and retain the tethered ARM/M bridge mode. Conservative missing-echo stops can limit usefulness; check hardware sensor behavior before changing firmware. Host neural inference and onboard distillation are different deployment modes. Physical accuracy, learned navigation and transfer are unmeasured until you perform real trials.
